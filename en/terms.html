Before you begin

SpiritByte is a local tool. Safeguard your keys and keep verified backups: Octa_Dev cannot unlock your vault or replace a lost password.

Using the application

Use the application to store and manage information you are authorized to handle. Do not use it to infringe the rights of others or carry out illegal activities. Review imported records and credentials before using them.

The application does not create an account with Octa_Dev and does not offer automatic synchronization. The current version's features do not include biometric unlocking or Android autofill.

Safekeeping of keys and recovery

You are responsible for choosing and safeguarding your master password, the 12-word recovery phrase and your backup passwords. The phrase is shown during setup: keep it in a safe place, separate from the device where possible.

Octa_Dev does not know your keys and cannot unlock or recover your vault. If you lose both the master password and the recovery phrase, you may lose access to its data. The recovery phrase cannot open a backup file: that file requires its backup password.

Do not share passwords or recovery phrases by support email.

Backups and data loss

Make encrypted backups regularly and verify that you can import them before clearing data, changing devices or reinstalling the application. Keep a safe copy before updating or modifying an installation.

A backup is a file you manage yourself. Clearing SpiritByte's storage or uninstalling the application removes its local data, but does not remove exported files. Appearance preferences and backgrounds are not included in the vault backup.

Importing adds records to the destination vault. Importing the same backup multiple times may create duplicates. Review the result of each import.

Security and software limitations

Encryption reduces the risk of exposing stored data; it does not guarantee absolute protection. Security also depends on your chosen password, the state of the operating system, installed applications and access to the device.

A compromised device, clipboard use, voluntarily sending secrets or losing keys may expose information or prevent its recovery. The software may contain errors; a version identified as a development build should be evaluated and tested before entrusting critical information to it.
